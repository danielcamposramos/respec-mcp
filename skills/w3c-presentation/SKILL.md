# Presentations and public explainers

Lead with the audience's problem and the decision or feedback sought. Show the actual state of the work, distinguishing a proposal, a demonstrated prototype and an adopted standard. Link the underlying evidence so a short presentation remains reviewable.

Use accessible headings, readable contrast and text, descriptions for meaningful visuals and supporting material. Expand unfamiliar abbreviations and avoid idioms that obscure meaning for an international audience. Native-language explanation can be translated into clear English with the author's technical meaning preserved. Use W3C templates and branding only in the appropriate role; a participant's talk does not automatically speak for the institution.
